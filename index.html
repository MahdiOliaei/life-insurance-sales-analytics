Business problem

The sales organization needed a transparent, reproducible way to evaluate a life-insurance campaign across multiple operational systems. New business and existing-portfolio collections carried different weights, while withdrawals, cancellations, and surrenders had to be considered separately. The final result had to be explainable to both management and the sales network.

1. Multiple source systems

Sales transactions, adjustment files, network master data, and branch-reference data had different structures and identifiers.

2. Rule-heavy scoring

Campaign-window dates, document cutoffs, 80/20 contribution weights, and eligibility rules had to be translated into repeatable logic.

3. Management usability

The output needed to go beyond ranking and surface concentration, productivity, exceptions, and data-quality issues.

Analytics workflow

Selected findings

Concentration is material

The top 10 sellers account for 77.1% of the weighted performance represented in this portfolio build.

Data reconciliation is part of the analysis

Sales and network-master records come from separate systems, so seller and branch standardization must precede any trustworthy ranking.

Gross sales alone are insufficient

The operating workflow includes 6,395 adjustment records across withdrawals, surrenders, and cancellations.

Illustrative SQL used in the portfolio-safe demo 
WITH ranked AS (
  SELECT
    seller_id,
    performance_index,
    network_share_pct,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
      ORDER BY performance_index DESC
    ) AS seller_rank
  FROM seller_performance
)
SELECT *
FROM ranked
WHERE seller_rank <= 20;

What I would deliver to management

Executive dashboard

Performance, participation, productivity, concentration, and portfolio-quality KPIs.

Network scorecards

Seller and branch ranking with rule-level traceability and exclusion reasons.

Decision recommendations

Actions for concentration risk, low-productivity segments, data quality, and incentive design.

Portfolio-safe data design

No customer names, national IDs, policy numbers, contracts, real seller/branch identifiers, or company monetary totals are exposed. The public dataset contains anonymized entities plus normalized indices and percentages so that the analytical method is inspectable without disclosing confidential operational data.
